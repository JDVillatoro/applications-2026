# What the ads say about the teaching statement

Collected 2026-10-08 from the ads for the 28 jobs in `tracker.csv`, plus
Brandeis (apply, not in the tracker yet). Quotes are from the ads; everything
else is a summary. Deadlines and document lists live in `tracker.csv`.

## No teaching statement requested

- **Waterloo**: no statement, but one of the four letters must address teaching.
- **Maryland**: no statement listed. The ad wants "evidence of excellent
  accomplishments and promise in … teaching" and encourages candidates to
  describe their work on "a climate of inclusiveness".
- **Victoria**: asks for a *teaching dossier* ("documenting teaching skills")
  instead of a statement. See their entry below.
- **Franklin & Marshall**: asks for "a holistic statement on your approach to
  inclusive excellence in teaching and student success" instead of a teaching
  statement. In effect it is a teaching statement with an inclusion focus.
- **Mississippi State**: one combined "summary of research plans and teaching
  philosophy".

## Themes across the ads

How many of the 29 ads raise each theme anywhere: in the statement prompt, in
the job description or in the cover letter instructions. A theme counted
under "asked for in the TS" is one the ad says the teaching statement itself
should address.

| Theme | Asked for in the TS | Raised elsewhere in the ad |
|---|---|---|
| **Inclusive teaching, belonging, students of all backgrounds** | Williams, Holy Cross, Rutgers, Bates, Western Michigan, F&M (whole statement) | UW Bothell (anywhere in materials), Middlebury, Grinnell (cover letter), Michigan (fellowship theme), Maryland, Villanova, Victoria, Manitoba (separate EDI statement), Cal Poly (separate student success statement), Penn State, NC State |
| **Evidence of effectiveness** (evaluations, observations, numbers) | UW Bothell ("any available evidence of teaching excellence"), Rutgers ("numerical evidence", optional portfolio) | Michigan (evaluations from last 3 years, separate), F&M (evaluations/observations, separate), Western Michigan (evidence document), Victoria (dossier), UT Austin ("positive evidence of excellence in mathematics education"), CMU ("sustained demonstrated excellence"), Maryland |

## Natural versions

Grouping by what the prompts ask for; you may well merge or split these.

1. **Research university, standard prompt.** Georgia Tech, JHU, LSU, Notre Dame,
   UT Austin, NC State, Binghamton, Michigan, Brandeis, Manitoba, Florida,
   Penn State (2 pp). Generic "teaching statement" or "teaching philosophy".
   Florida and Penn State also ask for *interests* (courses you could teach).
   Michigan is a Collegiate Fellows post about "access and inclusion", so for
   it the inclusion material matters more than the prompt suggests.
2. **Inclusive, evidence-based teaching named in the prompt.** Holy Cross,
   Bates (2 pp), Williams, Rutgers. All four want inclusive practice in the
   statement itself; Holy Cross and Bates add "evidence-based".
3. **Liberal arts colleges.** Williams, Middlebury, Grinnell, Bates, Holy Cross,
   F&M, Villanova. Undergraduate research, small classes and curriculum come
   up repeatedly; Middlebury asks directly for a liberal-arts vision.
4. **Teaching-focused jobs.** CMU, Victoria, Western Michigan, Cal Poly. They
   ask for teaching *contributions*, coordination, service courses and
   documented evidence rather than philosophy alone.
5. **Specific prompts.** TCU (style, techniques, assessment) and UW Bothell
   (evidence plus reflection, 3 pp) ask for things a generic statement may
   leave out.

## Per job, by deadline

**Holy Cross** (10-15). "A statement of your teaching philosophy and interests,
with particular emphasis on how you utilize evidence-based teaching strategies
to foster inclusive, high-belonging classroom environments." Wants interest
in Modern Algebra, Number Theory and/or Combinatorics; teaching "at all levels
of the curriculum"; working with students on undergraduate research; 3-2
load. The cover letter covers the Jesuit mission.

**Georgia Tech** (10-15). Plain "teaching statement". The job description
mentions curriculum development "for training a STEM focused student body"
and teaching across campus.

**Grinnell** (10-15). The ad lists no separate prompt for the teaching
statement, but belonging goes in the *cover letter* ("how the candidate will
contribute to creating a sense of belonging for all students in mathematics,
especially students who are underrepresented in the discipline"). Special
interest in "algebra and the transition to advanced mathematical thinking";
"mathematics is accessible to all students"; a collaborative department that
shapes curriculum together.

**Johns Hopkins** (10-15). Plain "teaching statement". No cover letter.

**LSU** (10-15). "A statement on teaching philosophy." Wants commitment to
graduate and undergraduate education.

**Notre Dame** (10-15). Plain "teaching statement".

**Williams** (10-15). "This should address your teaching philosophy and
experience, ways in which you foster an inclusive learning environment, and
other reflections or relevant information you would like to share." Also:
"engaging undergraduates in their research programs"; Winter Study
(January term) teaching; the Rice Center for Teaching.

**UW Bothell** (10-16, max 3 pp). "No more than three pages, that includes any
available evidence of teaching excellence, reflection on previous teaching and
how that informs current and future teaching plans." Across all materials:
"how [your] past experiences and/or future plans in research, teaching,
mentoring, and/or service promote a sense of belonging and foster a
welcoming and inclusive environment." About 40% of students are
first-generation. Courses: calculus, linear algebra, differential equations,
analysis, abstract algebra, numerical analysis, probability and statistics.
Curricular need is greatest in applied math and data science.

**Middlebury** (10-21). "A teaching statement that includes your vision for
teaching in a liberal arts setting." Throughout the materials: how your
teaching would connect with their concentrations in mathematics, applied
mathematics *and statistics*, and how your teaching "would support our
commitment to an open and supportive learning, teaching, and working
environment". Also First Year Seminars, Winter Term, and "cross-disciplinary
electives". Two of the three letters must speak to teaching.

**Cal Poly (Solano campus)** (10-23). Plain teaching statement, plus a separate
Universal Student Success Statement. The ad stresses the "service
curriculum", "creative teaching approaches", a teacher-scholar model and
"student success".

**UT Austin** (10-23). Plain "teaching statement". The ad wants "positive
evidence of excellence in mathematics education" and "excellence in
mentoring through teaching, research, and service".

**Maryland** (10-30). No statement listed (see top).

**NC State** (10-31). Teaching statement. "Commitment to excellence in
teaching at the undergraduate and graduate levels"; "a collegial and
supportive environment for all students".

**Mississippi State** (11-01). One combined "summary of research plans and
teaching philosophy". Teaching undergraduate and graduate courses; leading
M.S. students.

**Penn State** (11-01, max 2 pp). "A statement of your teaching philosophy and
interests." The job description mentions mentoring "at both the
undergraduate and graduate levels" and supervising undergraduate research.
The teaching letter must address "teaching experience and effectiveness in
detail".

**Rutgers** (11-01). "Teaching statement, including discussion of inclusive
teaching practices." Optional teaching portfolio; "Candidates are encouraged
to submit numerical evidence of teaching effectiveness." Graduate teaching;
the research-active load is 2-1.

**TCU** (11-01). "A teaching statement specifically addressing your teaching
style, classroom techniques, and assessment strategies." The teaching letter
should address teaching "in detail". The department has majors in Math,
Actuarial Science and Data Science, and a small PhD program.

**Villanova** (11-01). "Statement of Teaching Philosophy." Endowed
undergraduate research program; small classes; teaching development grants;
teaching at introductory, upper and graduate (MA) levels. Finalists write a
separate statement on Villanova's mission.

**Western Michigan** (11-01, max 2 pp). "Teaching statement of no more than two
pages that addresses your approach to teaching the student of all
backgrounds in service courses." There is also a separate 2-page leadership
statement on leading a multi-section course: instructional quality, faculty
development, assessment, working with graduate students and adjunct
instructors, and continuous course improvement. They want evidence of
"successful and innovative mathematics teaching", "AI or other technologies
in the classroom", "student engagement, success, and achievement", and
non-STEM and pre-algebra/algebra courses (MATH 1090–1160). The cover letter
must answer each qualification point by point.

**Bates** (11-08, max 2 pp). "Should describe how the applicant meets Bates'
standards of excellence in terms of inclusive and evidence-based pedagogy"
(Article II of the faculty handbook). Experience supporting a diverse student
body can go in the teaching statement or in a separate document. Courses:
Calculus, Linear Algebra, Multivariable, Introduction to Abstraction (proofs),
Differential Equations, upper-level courses, general education; advising and
thesis supervision.

**Binghamton** (11-08). Plain teaching statement. "Strong record of research
and teaching"; first-generation and international students.

**Franklin & Marshall** (11-09). The "holistic statement on your approach to
inclusive excellence in teaching and student success" takes the place of a
teaching statement. Teaching evaluations and/or observations are a separate
document. Courses: calculus, linear algebra, introduction to proof, real
analysis, and advanced topics building on analysis; new electives;
undergraduate research; the Connections general-education program.

**Brandeis** (11-01). Plain teaching statement; "a commitment to teaching". The
load is three courses a year.

**CMU** (11-15). "A statement describing teaching contributions and
philosophy." Preference for "sustained demonstrated excellence in teaching",
guiding undergraduate research, and "educational initiatives in the
Department and across the University". Two of the three letters must address
teaching.

**Florida** (11-15). "A statement of teaching experience and interests", so
lean on experience and the courses you'd teach rather than philosophy.
Supervising graduate and undergraduate research.

**Manitoba** (11-15). Plain teaching statement, plus a separate EDI statement.
Undergraduate and graduate teaching; "Indigenous perspectives" appear in the
university description.

**Michigan (LSA Collegiate Fellow)** (11-15). Plain teaching statement, plus
teaching evaluations "from all courses taught in the last three years". The
fellowship is for people "who have engaged in efforts to increase access and
inclusion in higher education through their teaching and mentoring" (or
research, or service).

**Victoria** (12-01). Teaching dossier instead of a statement. Workload is 70%
teaching, 20% service, 10% scholarship; coordinating first- and second-year
courses; curriculum development, "innovative pedagogy", outreach, or
Scholarship of Teaching & Learning. Wants "documented evidence of excellence"
in first- and second-year courses, ability to teach students from many
departments, and math *and* statistics teaching (an asset).
